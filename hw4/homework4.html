4.1 — Welford’s Recurrence Formulas for Mean and Variance

1. Mathematical Derivation

Computing variance using the naive formula Var(X) = E[X2] - (E[X])2 suffers from catastrophic numerical cancellation when dealing with large datasets or small variances. Welford's algorithm updates the mean x̄k and sum of squared differences M2,k online in a single pass:

M2,k = ∑i=1..k (xi - x̄k)2

By expanding M2,k - M2,k-1 and substituting x̄k = x̄k-1 + (xk - x̄k-1) / k, we derive the recurrence relation:

δ = xk - x̄k-1
x̄k = x̄k-1 + δ / k
δ2 = xk - x̄k
M2,k = M2,k-1 + δ · δ2

The sample variance is then: s2 = M2,k / (k - 1).

Statistical Interpretations & Inferential Insights

1. Unbiased Estimators in Inferential Statistics

The average of the m sampling means aligns with μ, and the average of the m sample variances aligns with σ2. This proves empirically that the sample mean X̄ and sample variance s2 (with Bessel's correction n-1) are unbiased estimators of the population parameters.

2. Impact of System Count (n) on Variability

Increasing n drastically reduces the spread (variance) of the sample means across the hackers, scaling proportionally to σ2/n. As n → ∞, individual hacker trajectories converge tightly to μ, demonstrating the Law of Large Numbers.
