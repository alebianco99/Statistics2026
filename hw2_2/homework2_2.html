Homework 2: Cryptographic Schemes, Statistical Cryptanalysis and Key Space Vulnerabilities

1. Theoretical Background & Scheme Comparison

Cryptographic schemes can be broadly categorized by their underlying security assumptions and key space structures:

  • The Caesar Cipher: A classical symmetric substitution cipher where each letter in the plaintext is shifted by a fixed key $K \in \{0, \dots, 25\}$. It preserves the statistical frequency distribution of the underlying language, making it vulnerable to frequency analysis.
  • Toy Elliptic Curve Encryption (ECC ElGamal-style): A modern asymmetric scheme based on the hardness of the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (ECDLP) over $\mathbb{F}_{17}$. A private key $k$ defines a public key $Q = k \cdot G$. Encryption uses an ephemeral scalar $r$ to generate $C_1 = rG$ and $C_2 = M + rQ$, masking the message without preserving language statistics.

4. Comparative Analysis & Vulnerability Discussion

1. Statistical Attack (Caesar) vs. Structural Attack (Toy ECC)

The attack on the Caesar cipher succeeds by exploiting natural language statistical redundancy. It measures the letter frequency distribution of the ciphertext and aligns it with the expected language profile.

In contrast, the attack on the Toy ECC scheme does not rely on language statistics (the output points of ECC appear pseudo-randomly distributed). Instead, it succeeds purely because the key space is artificially restricted ($|K| = 18$), allowing exhaustive search over the Discrete Logarithm Problem.

2. Feasibility on Properly Configured ECC

In a standard production curve like secp256k1, the order of the base point $n$ is a 256-bit prime number:

n \approx 1.1579 \times 10^{77}

Evaluating $1.1579 \times 10^{77}$ operations via brute force or Pollard's rho algorithm ($O(\sqrt{n}) \approx 2^{128}$ steps) exceeds the energy capacity of all computing systems on Earth combined. Thus, the vulnerability of our Toy ECC lies solely in its restricted parameter size, not in the fundamental mathematics of the curve.
