Homework 3: Stochastic Penetration Processes — LLN, CLT, and Penetration Score Normalization

1. Theoretical Foundations & Stochastic Modeling

We model a penetration attack against n systems (or steps) by m independent attackers using a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables X1, X2, ..., Xn.

  • Bernoulli Model: Xi ∈ {0, 1} with P(Xi = 1) = p. Represents success (penetration) or failure at each step.
    Mean E[Xi] = p, Variance Var(Xi) = p(1 - p).
  • Rademacher Model: Xi ∈ {-1, +1} with P(Xi = +1) = P(Xi = -1) = 1/2. Represents a symmetric zero-mean process.
    Mean E[Xi] = 0, Variance Var(Xi) = 1.

Normalized Quantities & Limit Theorems

We evaluate three distinct transformations of the cumulative score sequence:

1. Total Score: Sn = ∑ Xi   |   Var(Sn) = n · σ2 → ∞
2. Average Score: Sn / n = (1/n) ∑ Xi   |   Var(Sn/n) = σ2 / n → 0 (Law of Large Numbers)
3. Normalized Score: Sn / √n = (1/√n) ∑ Xi   |   Var(Sn/√n) = σ2 → N(0, 1) (Central Limit Theorem)

3. Comparative Analysis & Cybersecurity Interpretation

1. Law of Large Numbers (LLN) & Average Score Behavior

As step count n increases, the average score Sn/n collapses onto the theoretical expected value E[Xi]. The variance scales as σ2/n, tending to zero. In a cybersecurity context, this means that as an attacker targets a large number of systems, their overall empirical success rate stabilizes precisely at their intrinsic probability of compromise.

2. Central Limit Theorem (CLT) & The Scaling Factor √n

Dividing the total score by n causes the variance to shrink to zero, while looking at the unscaled total score Sn causes the variance to grow linearly to infinity. The scaling factor 1/√n is the precise mathematical normalization that stabilizes the variance at σ2 = 1. Under Rademacher increments, the discrete binomial distribution of Sn/√n converges rapidly to a standard Gaussian distribution N(0, 1).

3. Impact of System Size (n) and Number of Attackers (m)

  • System Size (n): Controls the convergence rate toward asymptotic limits. Larger n smooths individual sample paths and sharpens the bell-curve alignment for the normalized score.
  • Number of Attackers (m): Controls the sample size of the empirical distribution. A larger m populates the final histogram smoothly, verifying LLN and CLT across a population of threat actors.
